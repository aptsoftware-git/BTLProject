Quarterly Report: Proofly Product Update

Proofly are a document proofreading tool that help writers catch mistake before they publish. Last quater, the team have shipped several improvments to the pipeline, including a new severity scoring system and a redesigned interface.

The engineering team, lead by Sanjukta, focused on three main goals. Firstly, reducing the amount of false positives generated by the grammer checker. Secondly, improving the speed of the local LLM review stage, which was previously taking to long for larger documents. Thirdly, they wanted to make the accept and reject workflow feel more smoother for end users.

During testing, several bugs was discovered. One issue involved the mark elements in the annotated HTML being sorted different then the issues array returned by the API, which could of caused suggestion cards to point at the wrong highlight. Another issue was that the severity calculation didnt account for punctuation errors correctly, so alot of comma splices were being marked as low severity when they should of been medium.

The team also recieved feedback from early testers. Most people said the tool was very usefull, however a few users complained that the UI felt sluggish when there document had more then fifty issues on a single page. Its worth noting that performance on shorter documents were consistently fast.

Looking forward, the roadmap include adding support for exporting a corrected docx file that reflects the users accept and reject decisions. There is also plans to let users adjust the sensitivity of the grammar checker, so peoples who prefer a lighter touch can avoid getting flooded with suggestions.

In conclusion, the project have made good progress this quarter, and the team are optimistic about whats coming next. Their excited to see how the new severity coloring and accept/reject features perform once its tested on real world documents.
